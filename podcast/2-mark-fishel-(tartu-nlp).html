Kuidas ChatGPT päriselt töötab? Mis on sellega seonduvad ohud ja võimalused? Kas ChatGPTl on teadvus? Kas me seisame eksistentsiaalse ohu ees?

Kui on keegi siin Eestis, kellel on hulgaliselt kogemust ja intuitsiooni, et antud küsimustel arutleda, siis on see Mark Fišel. Mark on Tartu Ülikoolis Keeletehnoloogia Rakendusuuringute keskuse, lühidalt Tartu NLP, juht ja keeletehnoloogia professor. Tema juhitud meeskonnal on hulgaliselt kogemusi keeletehnoloogiate väljaarendamisel.

Viimaste uudiste valguses, kus teadlased, töösturid ja poliitikud üle maailma on näidanud erinevaid algatusi ChatGPT laadsete arenduste piiramiseks, on oluline, et me peaks selle üle avalikku debatti ja et seal debatis osaleksid ka inimesed, kellel on sügav teadmine antud teemadest. Kui koroona kriisis pakkusid ühiskonnale selgust Irja Lutsar, Karmen Joller ja Arkadi Popov — kõik oma ala eksperdid —, siis siin on minu püüe tuua teieni samasugune diskussioon, aga tehisintellekti teemal. How does ChatGPT really work? What risks and opportunities does it bring? Is ChatGPT conscious? Are we facing an existential threat?

If anyone in Estonia has the experience and intuition to discuss these questions, it is Mark Fišel. Mark is a professor of natural language processing at the University of Tartu and heads its Centre for Applied Language Technology Research, known as Tartu NLP. His team has extensive experience developing language technologies.

With scientists, business leaders, and politicians around the world recently proposing ways to restrict developments like ChatGPT, it is important to have a public debate that includes people with deep knowledge of the subject. During the coronavirus crisis, Irja Lutsar, Karmen Joller, and Arkadi Popov—all experts in their fields—helped society make sense of events. This is my attempt to bring you a similar discussion about AI. Even if we do not know exactly what the future holds, I certainly want to know what Mark Fišel thinks about it.

In the second half of the conversation, I explore the future of Estonian in a world where the most capable and convenient AI tools are developed in Silicon Valley or London. If I put on the hat of a hard-nosed capitalist and considered only business potential, Estonia's small market would probably not be a top priority for companies like Google and Facebook. But perhaps small languages such as Estonian, Komi, and Livonian can enrich the development of language technology in other ways?

Here is my conversation with Mark. Enjoy!
